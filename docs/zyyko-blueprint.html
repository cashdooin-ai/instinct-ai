zyyko.com · strategy blueprint · October 2026

Ask once. Local shops compete. You pick.

Zyyko connects Indian buyers with local businesses through AI. A person asks on WhatsApp, in ChatGPT, in Claude, or on zyyko.com: "Samsung 1.5 ton AC under ₹40k, installed this week, Indore." Zyyko sends the request to the matching nearby shops. They reply with a live price and availability in one tap, and the buyer gets the best three offers in minutes. The buyer pays with UPI inside WhatsApp, or books the service. The shop gets a real customer instead of a junk lead.

Verdict

This is a better business than an Instinct clone, and worth testing. You don't fight ChatGPT and Gemini. You supply them with the one thing they don't have: live local prices and stock from Indian shops, plus a way to order. Justdial shows local businesses will pay to reach buyers: ₹1,214 Cr FY26 revenue and 6.39 lakh paid campaigns (reported).

The hard part isn't the AI. It's getting shops to respond quickly, one city and one category at a time. Prove that cheaply before building much.

The idea in one picture

Most local search tells you who exists. Zyyko tells you who has it, at what price, today. Shops compete for each request, which suits how Indians already shop: compare, bargain, pick the best deal.

The shop-side reply uses a WhatsApp form (WhatsApp Flows), so a shopkeeper can answer in under 15 seconds. They can also reply with a voice note, which Zyyko turns into a structured offer.

Why this can beat ChatGPT, Google and Justdial

Live prices that AI doesn't have

ChatGPT, Gemini and Google know online prices and listings. None of them knows what the dealer 1 km away will sell at today, or which plumber is free at 6 pm. Zyyko gets that by asking shops in real time.

vs ChatGPT / Gemini / Google AI Mode

Be the supplier to every AI, not a rival

Publish Zyyko as a ChatGPT app, a Claude connector and a custom MCP server for Grok. When someone asks any AI for a local deal, the AI calls Zyyko. You win wherever people search.

vs trying to out-chat the giants

Shops pay only for real customers

Justdial sells upfront packages and sends the same lead to many sellers, and owners complain about junk calls. Zyyko charges a small fee only when a buyer picks that shop, or a commission when they pay.

vs Justdial, IndiaMART

Shops compete, so buyers save

On appliances, furniture, hardware and services, local quotes often vary 10–20% (assumption to test). A clear "you saved ₹2,400" moment is what gets people to come back and share.

vs fixed-price apps

Works with Meta's free shop bot

Meta Business Agent (India, July 2026) gives each shop a free AI that replies to its own customers. It doesn't bring new buyers or compare shops. Zyyko sends demand, and their bot can handle the follow-up.

Complement, not rival

WhatsApp-safe by design

Zyyko is a marketplace where AI helps buyers find and order. That's commerce, which the WhatsApp Business rules allow. It isn't a general-purpose chatbot, which they ban. Keep it strictly about shopping and services.

Lessons from those who failed

  • PhonePe Pincode spent about ₹90 Cr on an ONDC local-shopping app and wound it down in Dec 2025. Buyer discounts cost more than the low seller fees brought in. Lesson: don't buy growth with discounts. Savings should come from shops competing, not from your wallet.
  • Lookup (Khosla-backed, 2015–16) let people chat with local shops through human operators. It was sold to NowFloats. Lesson: people-run chat commerce costs too much per request. AI now brings that cost to a few rupees, which is why this can work in 2026.
  • Dunzo ran "anything delivered" and collapsed under logistics costs. Lesson: don't own delivery. Shops deliver, the buyer picks up, or you use partners like Porter, Shadowfax or ONDC logistics.
  • Justdial's complaints: shops pay upfront for leads shared with competitors. Lesson: charge for outcomes, and send each request only to a few well-rated shops.

Product

For buyers (free)

  • Ask by voice note or text in Hindi, English or another Indian language, on WhatsApp, ChatGPT, Claude or zyyko.com.
  • Get the top 3 live offers with price, distance, rating and delivery or install time.
  • Pay in chat with WhatsApp Pay (UPI, cards, other UPI apps through Razorpay or PayU), pay an advance, or pay at the shop.
  • Track the order, rate the shop, and use one "Zyyko guarantee" number if something goes wrong.

For shops

  • 2-minute sign-up on WhatsApp: send a shop photo, location and what you sell. AI builds the profile and catalogue from photos and voice.
  • Free: receive matching requests and reply with a price in one tap.
  • Pay only on success: a fee per chosen offer, or a commission on paid orders.
  • Zyyko Pro (paid): see more requests, a price-insight dashboard ("your AC price is 6% above the area average"), catalogue sync, and repeat-customer messages.

For AI platforms and apps

  • A Zyyko server exposing three tools: search_local_offers, request_quotes and place_order. It's listed in the ChatGPT app directory and the Claude connectors directory, and Grok users can add it as a custom MCP server.
  • Later: a "Zyyko API" that other apps (banks, telcos, super-apps) pay for per order.

Plan

Phase 0: prove it by hand (6 weeks, about ₹4–6 lakh)

  1. One city and two categories (appliances and home services). Sign up 300 shops by visiting them.
  2. A WhatsApp Business number with AI help. Ops staff send requests to shops and gather offers, partly by hand.
  3. Get buyers through local Click-to-WhatsApp ads, society WhatsApp groups and shop QR codes. Target 1,000 requests.
Go / no-go Continue only if: 60%+ of requests get 2 or more offers within 30 minutes · 25%+ of requests end in a purchase or booking · buyers save 8%+ against the online or first price on appliances · 50%+ of shops that won a customer agree to pay the per-offer fee · 30%+ of buyers come back within 60 days.

Phase 1: build the product (months 2–6)

  • WhatsApp Flows for shop replies, WhatsApp Pay checkout, order tracking, ratings.
  • The Zyyko MCP server, ChatGPT app and Claude connector, plus automated pages on zyyko.com.
  • Shop dashboard and Zyyko Pro; turn on fees.

Phase 2: expand (months 6–18)

  • Add furniture, hardware, auto and events. Open 3–5 cities using the same playbook.
  • ONDC buyer-app registration; delivery partners; API for other apps.
  • Raise a seed round on real numbers: requests, offer speed, conversion and shop revenue.
